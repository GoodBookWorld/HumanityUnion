# Humanity Union Institution Foundation Standard

## Version 1.0

### The Universal Minimum Requirements for Every Humanity Union Institution

---

# Document Purpose

Every Humanity Union institution should share a common architectural foundation.

The **Institution Foundation Standard** ensures consistency without preventing diversity. Institutions may differ greatly in function while sharing the same principles of responsibility, transparency, and accountability.

This document does **not** describe any specific institution. It establishes the universal institutional standard that every existing and future institution must satisfy.

This standard defines only the **minimum foundation**. Institution-specific capabilities, procedures, and organizational structures must be defined separately when justified by future Member signals.

This is an architectural specification. It does not prescribe implementation, storage, APIs, or interface technology.

# 1. Foundational Principles

Every Humanity Union institution is governed by principles that preserve legitimacy, limit authority, and enable responsible evolution.

## 1. Purpose before authority

An institution exists to carry defined public responsibility—not to acquire status, privilege, or unbounded power.

## 2. Responsibility before power

Institutional capability must follow documented responsibility. Power without assigned accountability is prohibited.

## 3. Transparency before legitimacy

Public understanding of purpose, mandate, and activity must precede claims of institutional legitimacy.

## 4. Participation before permanence

Meaningful Member participation and review must precede durable institutional expansion or permanence.

## 5. Review before expansion

No institution may expand its scope, authority, or structure without governed review and documented justification.

## 6. Institutional continuity through Institutional Memory

Institutions must preserve and contribute to Institutional Memory so civic knowledge survives changes in participants and leadership.

## 7. Member-driven evolution

Future institutional development must respond to demonstrated need, Member proposals, and governed review—not to internal assumption alone.

# 2. Minimum Institutional Elements

Every institution must define the following minimum elements. Absence of any element constitutes an incomplete institutional foundation.

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| **Public Name** | A clear, publicly identifiable designation |
| **Purpose** | The continuing civic reason the institution exists |
| **Public Description** | An accessible explanation of what the institution does and why |
| **Mandate** | The authorized scope of institutional authority and responsibility |
| **Scope** | The boundaries within which the institution may operate |
| **Responsibilities** | Documented duties the institution is expected to carry |
| **Explicit Limitations** | Actions, domains, and authorities the institution must not assume |
| **Relationship to other institutions** | How the institution coordinates with, differs from, and defers to other structures |
| **Relationship to Members** | How Members may observe, participate in, and hold the institution accountable |
| **Relationship to Working Groups** | How collaborative work connects to institutional responsibility |
| **Relationship to Institutional Memory** | How the institution preserves, contributes to, and respects institutional knowledge |
| **Review Conditions** | When and under what circumstances the institution must be evaluated |
| **Closure Conditions** | When and under what circumstances the institution may suspend or close |

These elements constitute the universal foundation. They do not predetermine internal organization, specialized capabilities, or future institutional design.

# 3. Mandate Standard

Every institution must maintain a **Mandate** that clearly identifies what the institution exists to accomplish, what it may do, what it must do, what it must not do, and which responsibilities belong elsewhere.

## Mandate Requirements

A valid mandate must be:

- **Public** — Available for Member examination
- **Specific** — Sufficiently defined to distinguish authorized from unauthorized action
- **Limited** — Narrow enough to prevent unbounded institutional growth
- **Reviewable** — Subject to periodic evaluation and governed amendment
- **Traceable** — Connected to formation rationale and Institutional Memory

No mandate should remain undefined, implied, or dependent on informal understanding alone.

An institution may not operate outside its mandate or reinterpret its mandate without governed review.

# 4. Responsibility Standard

Institutional **Responsibilities** define what the institution is expected to carry on behalf of the civic community.

## Responsibility Requirements

Responsibilities should be:

- **Documented** — Publicly recorded and attributable
- **Observable** — Capable of being examined through Activities and records
- **Reviewable** — Subject to Member evaluation and institutional review
- **Limited** — Confined to the institution's mandate and scope
- **Traceable** — Connected to formation rationale, Activities, and Institutional Memory

Responsibilities should **never** depend solely on individuals. Institutional duty must survive participant turnover and must not collapse into personal authority.

When responsibilities overlap with other institutions, the overlap must be documented and subject to coordination—not left ambiguous.

---

# 5. Boundary Standard

Every institution must define explicit boundaries. **Undefined authority is prohibited.**

## Required Boundaries

| Boundary Type | Definition |
|---------------|------------|
| **Operational boundaries** | What work the institution may perform and what domains it may enter |
| **Decision boundaries** | What decisions the institution may make and what decisions belong elsewhere |
| **Information boundaries** | What information the institution may collect, share, restrict, or publish |
| **Coordination boundaries** | How the institution may interact with other institutions, Working Groups, and civic structures |
| **Accountability boundaries** | To whom and through what mechanisms the institution remains answerable |

Boundaries must be documented, publicly accessible, and reviewable. Expansion of any boundary requires governed proposal and review—not informal institutional action.

---

# 6. Participation Standard

Every institution must identify who may participate, who may contribute, who carries responsibility, and how Members may interact with the institution.

## Participation Requirements

Institutions should define:

- **Participation scope** — Who may engage in institutional work within mandate limits
- **Contribution pathways** — How Members and collaborators may offer input, expertise, or evidence
- **Responsibility assignment** — Who currently carries institutional accountability for defined duties
- **Member interaction** — How Members may observe, question, support, or challenge institutional activity

This standard does **not** define future constitutional procedures, electoral systems, or permanent leadership structures.

Participation must remain open to Member scrutiny. Provisional or founding participation must not be treated as permanent control without governed review.

# 7. Transparency Standard

Institutions must operate with default transparency. Restrictions require justification.

## Transparency Requirements

Every institution should publicly explain:

- **Purpose** — Why the institution exists
- **Mandate** — What it is authorized to do and not do
- **Activities** — What institutional work has occurred and is occurring
- **Responsibilities** — What duties the institution carries
- **Reports** — Periodic or event-driven summaries of institutional performance and public value
- **Review status** — When review occurred, is occurring, or is due
- **Known limitations** — Constraints, gaps, risks, and unresolved needs

Transparency is a condition of institutional legitimacy—not an optional feature.

Information restrictions must be documented, justified, limited in scope, and subject to review.

# 8. Accountability Standard

Every institution must remain accountable through Activities, Institutional Memory, the Decision Lifecycle, Member review, and periodic evaluation.

## Accountability Mechanisms

| Mechanism | Role |
|-----------|------|
| **Activities** | Preserve a traceable record of institutional work |
| **Institutional Memory** | Retain formation rationale, mandate history, and lessons learned |
| **Decision Lifecycle** | Govern formal institutional decisions with transparency and review |
| **Member review** | Enable attributable Member evaluation of institutional performance |
| **Periodic evaluation** | Require scheduled assessment of mandate fit, public value, and continued need |

Accountability is continuous—not limited to formation or crisis.

An institution cannot exempt itself from accountability, suppress its Activity record, or operate without review conditions.

# 9. Review Standard

Every institution must define when review occurs, what is reviewed, and possible review outcomes.

## Review Requirements

Review is **mandatory** regardless of institutional age, reputation, or perceived success.

Each institution must specify:

- **Review timing** — Scheduled intervals, founding-period deadlines, or condition-triggered review
- **Review scope** — Mandate fit, public value, participation, boundaries, risks, duplication, and continued need
- **Review outcomes** — Possible results including continuation, mandate revision, provisional return, merger, transformation, suspension, or closure

Review must produce documented findings connected to Activities and Institutional Memory.

Future procedures may define how review outcomes are formally decided. This standard establishes only that review is required and must be structured.

Reference: [15_INSTITUTION_FORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md](./15_INSTITUTION_FORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md)

# 10. Evolution Standard

Institutional development requires Member proposals, evidence, public discussion, review, and documented justification.

## Evolution Requirements

Institutions may **not** evolve through internal decisions alone.

Any proposed change to institutional capability, structure, mandate, or authority must explain:

- the demonstrated need;
- the expected public value;
- the effect on institutional responsibility;
- the risks and effects on other institutions;
- the evidence supporting the change.

Evolution must follow governed pathways—not informal accumulation of power, function, or permanence.

Institution-specific capabilities, procedures, and organizational structures require separate proposals when justified by demonstrated operational need.

Reference: [15_INSTITUTION_FORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md](./15_INSTITUTION_FORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md)

# 11. Relationship to Previous Architectures

Every Humanity Union institution operates inside the platform's architectural systems. No institution exists outside this civic infrastructure.

## Activity Engine

Reference: [05_ACTIVITY_ENGINE_SPECIFICATION.md](./05_ACTIVITY_ENGINE_SPECIFICATION.md)

Institutional work creates Activities. The Activity Engine preserves the traceable civic history of institutional action. Institutions must not rewrite or remove that history.

## Discussion and Collaboration

Reference: [06_DISCUSSION_AND_COLLABORATION_MODEL.md](./06_DISCUSSION_AND_COLLABORATION_MODEL.md)

Institutions participate in the civic Discussion ecosystem. Public reasoning, objection, and revision must remain accessible to Members.

## Working Groups

Reference: [08_WORKING_GROUPS_ARCHITECTURE.md](./08_WORKING_GROUPS_ARCHITECTURE.md)

Working Groups organize collaborative work around defined objectives. Institutions carry continuing formally recognized responsibility. The two forms complement one another; institutions must not absorb all collaborative activity by default.

## Decision Lifecycle

Reference: [12_DECISION_LIFECYCLE_ARCHITECTURE.md](./12_DECISION_LIFECYCLE_ARCHITECTURE.md)

Formal institutional decisions follow the Decision Lifecycle. This ensures transparency, reviewability, and legitimate authorization.

## Institution Formation

Reference: [15_INSTITUTION_FORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md](./15_INSTITUTION_FORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md)

Institutions enter the platform through governed formation. The Foundation Standard applies from provisional status onward and persists through recognition, evolution, and closure.

## Institutional Memory

Reference: [13_INSTITUTIONAL_MEMORY_ARCHITECTURE.md](./13_INSTITUTIONAL_MEMORY_ARCHITECTURE.md)

Institutions must preserve formation rationale, mandate history, review findings, and lessons learned. Institutional Memory ensures continuity beyond individual participants.

## Governance Integration

Reference: [14_GOVERNANCE_INTEGRATION_ARCHITECTURE.md](./14_GOVERNANCE_INTEGRATION_ARCHITECTURE.md)

Institutions operate within a coordinated governance ecosystem. Each institution must identify its role, boundaries, relationships, and information responsibilities relative to other institutions.

# 12. Non-Goals

This document does **not** define:

- specific Humanity Union institutions;
- departments, chambers, or internal hierarchies;
- elections or representation mechanisms;
- detailed internal procedures;
- constitutional powers;
- voting systems or approval thresholds;
- permanent leadership structures;
- complete institutional user interfaces;
- institution-specific operational functionality.

Existing conceptual institutions referenced elsewhere in the Blueprint are **not** treated as fully implemented structures by this document.

Institution-specific specifications remain the responsibility of future Member proposals justified by demonstrated need.

---

# 13. Guiding Principle

Every Humanity Union institution should share a common architectural foundation while remaining free to evolve according to demonstrated public need and meaningful Member participation.

The Foundation Standard ensures that all institutions—regardless of function, scale, or future development—operate with purpose, bounded authority, transparency, accountability, and governed review.

The platform provides the universal foundation.

Members provide the signal for what each institution should become.
