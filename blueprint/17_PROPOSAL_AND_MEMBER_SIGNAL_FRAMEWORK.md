# Proposal Framework

## Version 2.0

### The Proposal Evolution Architecture of Humanity Union

---

# Document Purpose

This document defines the universal Proposal Framework of the Humanity Union platform.

It specifies how ideas evolve into mature civic Proposals through open collaboration, evidence-based refinement, and collective analysis before entering formal public support and collective decision.

A Proposal is not merely a document.

It is the primary collaborative object through which Participants transform ideas into actionable civic initiatives.

Proposal development occurs entirely within **Collaborative Analysis**, where Participants collaboratively improve understanding, refine solutions, identify risks, and prepare Proposals for Petition.

This document replaces proposal models centered around institutions or individual authority.

Every Proposal follows the same architectural principles regardless of its subject, scale, or future implementation.

---

**Status:** Foundational Architectural Standard

**Scope:** Proposal creation, proposal evolution, collaborative refinement, proposal quality, proposal readiness, and transition to Petition

**Implementation:** Out of scope for this document

# 1. Foundational Philosophy

Every meaningful improvement begins with an idea.

Ideas become valuable only when they are examined, challenged, improved, and understood collectively.

Humanity Union does not seek to reward those who submit the largest number of Proposals.

Instead, it seeks to create the conditions in which the best Proposals naturally emerge through collaboration.

A Proposal should represent collective understanding rather than individual ambition.

Every Proposal should remain open to evidence, revision, and constructive criticism throughout its development.

Proposal evolution is therefore a process of continuous learning rather than competition.

---

# 2. Purpose of the Proposal Framework

The Proposal Framework establishes a universal method for transforming ideas into mature civic actions.

Its objectives are to:

- organize collaborative problem-solving;
- encourage evidence-based development;
- preserve proposal history;
- improve proposal quality;
- support transparent refinement;
- enable constructive disagreement;
- prepare Proposals for public support;
- reduce poorly developed initiatives entering collective decision.

The Proposal Framework exists to improve decisions before voting becomes necessary.

# 3. Core Design Principles

## Collaboration before authorship

The value of a Proposal comes from collaborative refinement rather than individual ownership.

Participants improve Proposals together.

---

## Quality before quantity

Submitting many Proposals should never be encouraged.

Improving existing Proposals is often more valuable than creating new ones.

---

## Evidence before opinion

Opinions initiate discussion.

Evidence strengthens Proposals.

Proposal evolution should increasingly rely on verified information, practical experience, and transparent reasoning.

---

## Evolution before permanence

A Proposal is expected to evolve.

Revision is a sign of improvement rather than weakness.

Participants should be encouraged to refine existing work whenever possible.

---

## Transparency before certainty

Proposal development should remain visible.

Participants should understand:

- what changed;
- why it changed;
- who contributed;
- which evidence influenced development.

---

## Participation before authority

Every Participant may contribute to Proposal development.

Institutional position does not determine the quality of an idea.

Better ideas should emerge through better collaboration.

---

# 4. Proposal

A Proposal is the primary collaborative object of Humanity Union.

It represents a structured recommendation intended to improve some aspect of society, governance, knowledge, technology, environment, security, or the Humanity Union ecosystem.

Every Proposal belongs to an Initiative.

A Proposal cannot exist independently from an Initiative.

---

## Purpose of a Proposal

A Proposal exists to describe:

- a problem;
- an opportunity;
- a desired outcome;
- a recommended solution;
- expected benefits;
- potential risks;
- implementation considerations.

Its purpose is to provide sufficient understanding for informed civic evaluation.

---

## Proposal Characteristics

Every Proposal should remain:

- transparent;
- evidence-informed;
- understandable;
- collaborative;
- traceable;
- continuously improvable.

Proposals should remain accessible to Participants regardless of technical expertise.

---

## Proposal Structure

Although implementation details may evolve, every Proposal should ultimately describe:

- objective;
- background;
- rationale;
- expected impact;
- supporting evidence;
- identified risks;
- implementation considerations;
- alternative approaches.

Additional fields may be introduced without changing the architectural model.

---

# 5. Proposal Lifecycle

Proposal development is continuous.

Every Proposal evolves through Collaborative Analysis before entering formal civic support.

The Proposal Lifecycle consists of the following stages:

```text
Idea
    ↓
Initial Proposal
    ↓
Collaborative Analysis
    ↓
Proposal Revision
    ↓
Proposal Maturity
    ↓
Petition
```

Each stage increases collective understanding.

Progression is based on proposal quality rather than elapsed time.

---

## Idea

Every Proposal begins as an idea.

Ideas may originate from:

- Participants;
- Discussions;
- Collective Signals;
- research;
- community needs;
- implementation experience;
- previous Initiatives;
- Impact reviews.

Ideas are intentionally lightweight.

Not every idea should become a Proposal.

---

## Initial Proposal

An Initial Proposal provides the first structured description of an idea.

Its purpose is to initiate collaboration rather than present a finished solution.

Initial Proposals are expected to contain uncertainty.

They exist to encourage constructive participation.

---

## Collaborative Analysis

Collaborative Analysis is where Proposal development primarily occurs.

Participants collectively improve:

- clarity;
- evidence;
- feasibility;
- completeness;
- implementation considerations;
- public understanding.

This stage represents the majority of Proposal evolution.

---

## Proposal Revision

Revision incorporates knowledge gained during Collaborative Analysis.

Every revision should improve one or more aspects of the Proposal.

Revision history remains permanently preserved.

Earlier versions continue contributing to Institutional Memory.

---

## Proposal Maturity

Proposal Maturity represents a state rather than a fixed milestone.

A mature Proposal demonstrates sufficient quality to be considered for public support.

Maturity does not imply perfection.

It indicates that meaningful collaborative refinement has taken place.

# 6. Proposal Ownership

Every Proposal has an identifiable origin.

However, ownership exists to preserve accountability and historical continuity rather than exclusive control.

Humanity Union encourages collaborative stewardship instead of individual possession.

A Proposal belongs to its Initiative and contributes to Humanity Union's Collective Knowledge.

---

## Original Author

The Participant who creates an Initial Proposal becomes its Original Author.

The Original Author establishes the Proposal's initial direction by:

- identifying the problem;
- describing the initial objective;
- providing the first rationale;
- initiating collaborative development.

The Original Author does not permanently control the Proposal.

---

## Collaborative Stewardship

Proposal evolution is a collaborative process.

Participants may improve a Proposal by:

- suggesting revisions;
- contributing evidence;
- identifying implementation concerns;
- proposing clarifications;
- improving structure;
- recommending alternatives;
- expanding supporting information.

The Proposal should reflect collective understanding rather than individual preference.

---

## Accountability

Every Proposal should preserve complete accountability.

Participants should always be able to determine:

- who created the Proposal;
- who contributed revisions;
- what changes occurred;
- when revisions occurred;
- why revisions were made.

Proposal accountability strengthens public trust.

---

## Proposal History

Proposal history must remain immutable.

The platform should preserve:

- original versions;
- subsequent revisions;
- supporting discussions;
- evidence history;
- related Collective Signals;
- Consensus Summaries;
- transition to Petition.

Proposal history represents the evolution of civic understanding rather than merely document versions.

---

# 8. Proposal Scope

Every Proposal should clearly define its intended scope.

Scope determines where implementation is expected rather than how collaboration occurs.

Collaborative Analysis remains identical regardless of Proposal scope.

---

## Possible Scopes

Examples include:

- Community;
- Municipality;
- City;
- Region;
- Province or State;
- Country;
- International;
- Global;
- Humanity Union Internal Governance.

Future scopes may be introduced as Humanity Union expands.

---

## Scope Evolution

Proposal scope may evolve during Collaborative Analysis.

Participants may determine that a Proposal originally intended for one community should instead be implemented:

- regionally;
- nationally;
- internationally;
- globally.

Likewise, global concepts may later be adapted into local implementations.

Scope evolution represents improved understanding rather than proposal failure.

---

# 9. Proposal Relationships

No Proposal exists in isolation.

Every Proposal forms part of a broader knowledge ecosystem.

Proposal relationships improve navigation, collaboration, and long-term institutional learning.

---

## Related Proposals

A Proposal may relate to other Proposals through:

- shared objectives;
- complementary solutions;
- alternative approaches;
- implementation dependencies;
- common evidence;
- similar risks.

Relationships help Participants avoid unnecessary duplication.

---

## Proposal Dependencies

Some Proposals depend upon the completion of others.

Examples include:

- legal reform before implementation;
- infrastructure before deployment;
- funding before execution;
- institutional creation before governance.

Dependencies improve implementation planning without restricting collaborative analysis.

---

## Alternative Proposals

Complex problems may have multiple legitimate solutions.

Alternative Proposals should coexist rather than compete for visibility.

Participants should compare:

- benefits;
- risks;
- feasibility;
- expected impact;
- implementation complexity.

Alternative approaches strengthen collective decision-making.

---

## Proposal Merging

Collaborative Analysis may reveal that multiple Proposals address the same objective.

Participants may voluntarily merge compatible Proposals into a stronger collaborative Proposal.

Proposal merging should preserve:

- contribution history;
- revision history;
- original authorship;
- supporting evidence.

Nothing should be lost during consolidation.

---

## Proposal Splitting

Some Proposals become too broad.

Participants may divide one Proposal into several specialized Proposals.

Splitting should improve:

- clarity;
- implementation planning;
- collaborative focus;
- evidence organization.

Proposal splitting should preserve complete historical traceability.

# 10. Collective Signals

Collective Signals are collaborative observations that emerge during Proposal development.

They are not submitted as individual opinions.

They emerge naturally from recurring patterns discovered throughout Collaborative Analysis.

Collective Signals help Participants recognize where collective understanding is developing, where uncertainty remains, and where additional collaboration may be valuable.

Collective Signals inform Proposal evolution.

They never determine Proposal outcomes.

---

## Purpose of Collective Signals

Collective Signals exist to strengthen Proposal quality by revealing important patterns that may not be immediately visible within individual Discussions.

Their objectives include:

- identifying recurring concerns;
- revealing common priorities;
- exposing evidence gaps;
- identifying implementation challenges;
- highlighting repeated improvement suggestions;
- detecting conflicting viewpoints;
- recognizing emerging consensus;
- identifying unresolved uncertainty.

Collective Signals improve awareness.

They never replace human judgment.

---

## Sources of Collective Signals

Collective Signals may emerge from:

- Discussions;
- Proposal revisions;
- Participant feedback;
- evidence analysis;
- implementation experience;
- Consensus Summaries;
- AI-assisted pattern recognition;
- cross-Initiative collaboration.

Signals represent accumulated collaborative knowledge rather than isolated opinions.

---

## Signal Evolution

Collective Signals continuously evolve.

A Signal may:

- emerge;
- strengthen;
- weaken;
- merge with another Signal;
- divide into multiple Signals;
- disappear after further clarification.

Signal evolution reflects the evolution of collective understanding.

---

## Signal Transparency

Every Collective Signal should remain fully transparent.

Participants should always understand:

- why the Signal exists;
- which collaborative activity contributed to it;
- which Discussions are related;
- which evidence supports it;
- where uncertainty remains.

Signals must never become opaque system-generated conclusions.

---

## Signals and Proposal Improvement

Collective Signals often identify opportunities for Proposal refinement.

Examples include:

- recurring requests for clarification;
- missing implementation details;
- repeated legal concerns;
- overlooked stakeholder groups;
- conflicting scientific evidence;
- unclear objectives;
- duplicated functionality;
- underestimated risks.

Signals encourage improvement.

Participants determine how improvement occurs.

---

## Signals and Proposal Prioritization

Collective Signals may help Participants identify which areas deserve additional attention during Collaborative Analysis.

Signals should never prioritize Proposals automatically.

Instead, they provide contextual awareness supporting better civic collaboration.

---

# 11. Proposal Quality

Proposal quality determines readiness for broader civic participation.

Quality should result from collaborative refinement rather than administrative approval.

Proposal quality is continuously improved throughout Collaborative Analysis.

---

## Objectives

High-quality Proposals should:

- clearly define the problem;
- describe realistic objectives;
- present understandable reasoning;
- include supporting evidence;
- acknowledge uncertainty;
- evaluate alternatives;
- identify implementation considerations;
- explain expected Impact.

Quality improves informed decision-making.

---

## Characteristics of High-Quality Proposals

A mature Proposal should be:

- understandable;
- transparent;
- evidence-informed;
- logically structured;
- practically achievable;
- collaboratively refined;
- historically traceable.

No Proposal is expected to be perfect.

Quality represents readiness rather than completeness.

---

## Evidence Quality

Proposal quality depends heavily upon evidence quality.

Evidence should preferably be:

- relevant;
- verifiable;
- transparent;
- current where appropriate;
- independently reviewable.

Conflicting evidence should remain visible whenever legitimate disagreement exists.

---

## Risk Identification

Every Proposal should attempt to identify potential risks.

Examples include:

- implementation complexity;
- financial uncertainty;
- technological limitations;
- legal conflicts;
- unintended consequences;
- ethical considerations;
- environmental impact;
- public safety concerns.

Identifying risks strengthens Proposal quality.

It should never discourage Proposal development.

---

## Alternative Solutions

Proposal quality improves when Participants examine alternative approaches.

Alternative solutions should be encouraged whenever they:

- address the same objective;
- reduce implementation complexity;
- improve effectiveness;
- reduce cost;
- reduce risk;
- improve inclusiveness.

Alternative analysis supports informed civic decisions.

---

## Continuous Improvement

Proposal quality is never static.

Every Proposal remains open to future improvement, including after implementation.

Lessons learned through Impact assessment may generate future Proposal revisions or entirely new Initiatives.

Proposal evolution therefore continues beyond implementation.

---

# 12. AI Facilitation

Artificial Intelligence supports Proposal development by improving organization, accessibility, and collaborative efficiency.

AI functions as an assistant.

It does not function as a decision-maker.

---

## AI Objectives

AI should assist Participants by:

- organizing information;
- reducing duplication;
- identifying related Proposals;
- improving navigation;
- summarizing collaboration;
- detecting recurring themes;
- identifying evidence relationships;
- supporting multilingual collaboration;
- improving accessibility.

AI enhances collaboration.

Participants remain responsible for civic judgment.

---

## AI Proposal Assistance

AI may assist Proposal development by:

- suggesting structural improvements;
- identifying incomplete sections;
- detecting duplicate proposals;
- identifying conflicting evidence;
- organizing references;
- generating draft summaries;
- highlighting unresolved questions;
- suggesting related Initiatives.

All AI-generated recommendations remain optional.

---

## AI Transparency

Participants should always know:

- when AI contributed;
- which information AI analyzed;
- what recommendations AI generated;
- what uncertainty remains.

AI recommendations should remain clearly distinguishable from Participant contributions.

---

## AI Limitations

Artificial Intelligence must never:

- create official Proposals independently;
- approve Proposal readiness;
- reject Participant contributions;
- suppress legitimate disagreement;
- determine Petition eligibility;
- determine Collective Decisions;
- replace Participant responsibility.

Every official Proposal remains a human civic artifact.

# 13. Proposal Readiness

Proposal Readiness represents the stage at which a Proposal has achieved sufficient collaborative maturity to enter the Petition phase.

Readiness does not indicate that a Proposal is universally accepted.

It indicates that the Proposal has undergone meaningful collaborative development and is ready for broader civic evaluation.

Proposal Readiness is determined by the quality of collaborative analysis rather than by popularity.

---

## Purpose of Proposal Readiness

Proposal Readiness exists to ensure that Proposals entering Petition are:

- understandable;
- evidence-informed;
- collaboratively refined;
- transparently documented;
- practically reviewable;
- sufficiently mature for public support.

Proposal Readiness improves the quality of civic participation by reducing premature or poorly developed Proposals.

---

## Readiness Indicators

A Proposal may be considered ready when it demonstrates:

- a clearly defined objective;
- an understandable description;
- documented rationale;
- meaningful collaborative revision;
- identified implementation considerations;
- supporting evidence where appropriate;
- documented alternative viewpoints;
- acknowledged uncertainty;
- preserved revision history.

No single indicator is sufficient on its own.

Readiness emerges from the overall maturity of collaborative development.

---

## Unresolved Questions

Proposal Readiness does not require every question to be resolved.

Some uncertainty may remain.

Remaining uncertainty should be:

- visible;
- documented;
- understandable;
- proportionate to the Proposal.

Transparency is preferable to artificial certainty.

---

## Minority Perspectives

Proposal Readiness does not require unanimous agreement.

Well-reasoned minority perspectives should remain attached to the Proposal whenever they contribute to informed civic evaluation.

Constructive disagreement strengthens Proposal quality.

---

## Proposal Completeness

Completeness should be evaluated proportionally.

A local Initiative may require less supporting documentation than a global constitutional reform.

Proposal complexity should remain appropriate to its intended scope.

---

# 14. Transition to Petition

Petition represents the transition from collaborative development to public civic support.

Petition is not the continuation of Proposal refinement.

It begins a new phase of the Initiative Lifecycle.

Collaborative Analysis prepares the Proposal.

Petition measures public support.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of Petition is to determine whether a collaboratively developed Proposal deserves formal consideration through a Decision Session.

Petition exists to:

- demonstrate civic interest;
- encourage public participation;
- increase transparency;
- identify community support;
- prepare Proposals for collective decision.

Petition does not determine the final outcome.

It determines whether the Proposal advances within the Initiative Lifecycle.

---

## Relationship to Collaborative Analysis

Collaborative Analysis and Petition serve different purposes.

Collaborative Analysis seeks understanding.

Petition seeks public support.

Proposal refinement primarily occurs before Petition.

Minor clarifications may continue during Petition, but fundamental redesign should normally occur through renewed Collaborative Analysis.

---

## Proposal Stability

Before entering Petition, a Proposal should achieve reasonable stability.

Participants should understand:

- what they are supporting;
- why the Proposal exists;
- how it evolved;
- what alternatives were considered;
- what uncertainty remains.

Stable Proposals improve public trust.

---

## Proposal Identity

Every Proposal maintains a continuous identity throughout its lifecycle.

Entering Petition does not create a new Proposal.

The same Proposal continues into the next phase while preserving:

- original authorship;
- revision history;
- collaborative contributions;
- evidence;
- Collective Signals;
- Consensus Summaries.

Institutional Memory remains continuous across every lifecycle stage.

---
